# Intent: Design a greenhouse experiment that distinguishes osmotic stress from sodium-specific toxicity in tomato seedlings while controlling
# - Treatment dose
# - Position effects
# - Measurement bias

[Question]: Does sodium chloride reduce seedling growth beyond the effect expected from reduced water potential alone

[Hypotheses]:
- Osmotic: Growth reduction is explained primarily by water-potential stress
- SodiumSpecific: Sodium chloride causes additional injury beyond a water-potential-matched nonionic treatment
- Underdetermined: The available design cannot separate the mechanisms cleanly

[Resources]:
- 120 genetically similar seedlings from the same planting cohort
- 4 greenhouse benches with measurable light and temperature gradients
- Experimental resources:
  - Irrigation solutions
  - Balance
  - Leaf-area imaging
  - Chlorophyll fluorometer
  - Tissue ion assay
- 21 days after treatment begins

[DesignRules]:
- Establish treatment water potentials from measured solutions rather than nominal solute concentration alone
- Include an untreated control and at least two matched stress levels for sodium chloride and a validated nonionic osmotic control
- Randomize plants within blocks defined by bench position
- Balance baseline plant size across treatments before randomization
- Predefine one primary growth endpoint and treat other measurements as secondary or mechanistic
- Blind image-based growth measurements to treatment identity where practical
- Record missing plants and protocol deviations rather than silently replacing them

=> Identify likely confounders:
  - Bench effects
  - Evaporative concentration
  - Unequal starting size
  - Osmotic-control side effects
=> Append the resulting protocol to [Design]

[InferencePlan]:
=> Compare each sodium-chloride treatment in [Design] with its water-potential-matched osmotic control
=> Estimate treatment effects with uncertainty while accounting for greenhouse blocks
=> Check whether tissue sodium tracks effects that remain after osmotic matching
=> Treat a difference as mechanism-discriminating only when treatment matching and measurement quality are adequate
=> If the osmotic control introduces its own material biological effect, report the mechanism comparison as limited rather than forcing a conclusion
