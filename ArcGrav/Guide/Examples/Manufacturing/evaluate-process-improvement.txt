# Intent: Compare manufacturing process improvements against throughput quality cost safety and implementation evidence without collapsing unlike consequences into a single score

[CellContext]:
- Process: CNC machining cell for aluminum valve bodies
- Current staffing: Two operators across three machines and final inspection
- Current accepted output: 132 parts per shift
- Required output: 150 parts per shift
- Primary defect: Burr remaining at cross-hole intersection
- Defect detection point: Final inspection after wash

[Baseline]:
- CNC1:
  - Average cycle: 128 seconds per part
  - First-pass yield: 99 percent
- CNC2:
  - Average cycle: 142 seconds per part
  - First-pass yield: 96 percent
- Wash:
  - Average cycle: 75 seconds per part
  - Batch capacity: Four parts
- Final inspection:
  - Average handling and inspection: 95 seconds per part
  - Rework return distance: 18 meters to CNC2 area
- Observed pattern:
  - Queue forms before final inspection during the middle of the shift
  - Burr defects are discovered after downstream handling and wash
  - CNC2 experiences short operator-wait events during insert checks

[Options]:
- InProcessGauge:
  - Change: Add a go-no-go burr probe and visual standard at CNC2 unload
  - Capital cost: 2400 dollars
  - Expected effect: Detect burr condition before wash and final inspection
  - Training need: One standardized work update for both operators
- DualInspectionBench:
  - Change: Add a second final inspection bench and duplicate gauges
  - Capital cost: 11200 dollars
  - Expected effect: Increase final inspection handling capacity
  - Training need: No new inspection method
- PresetInsertKits:
  - Change: Stage preset insert kits and replacement criteria at CNC2
  - Capital cost: 3600 dollars
  - Expected effect: Reduce operator-wait time during insert checks
  - Training need: Updated tool-life response standard

[DecisionCriteria]:
- Throughput:
  - Accepted parts per shift
  - Constraint relief at the system level
- Quality:
  - Defect prevention
  - Detection timing
  - Rework burden
- Cost:
  - Capital spend
  - Recurring consumables
  - Expected labor effect
- Safety:
  - Guarding integrity
  - Reach exposure
  - Manual handling burden
- Implementation:
  - Downtime required
  - Training complexity
  - Evidence needed before permanent adoption

[Nonnegotiable]:
- Do not reduce the approved critical-dimension inspection frequency
- Do not require manual access inside a guarded machine envelope during automatic operation
- Do not increase single-person lifting beyond the current ergonomic limit
- Do not exceed 15000 dollars of capital without separate approval
