# Intent: Rebalance a duel-arena ruleset where evasive mobility and a high-impact charged weapon compete for control without erasing their intended counterplay

[BalanceContext]:
- Mode: One-versus-one arena duel
- Round target: 90 to 150 seconds
- Skill focus: Positioning and timing
- Problem: High-level play favors repeated dash disengagement until HeavyCannon charge is safe

[CurrentRules]:
- Dash:
  - Charges: 2
  - Recharge time: 3.0 seconds per charge
  - Distance: 6 meters
  - Invulnerability window: 0.18 seconds
  - Recovery: 0.20 seconds
- HeavyCannon:
  - Charge time: 1.25 seconds
  - Projectile speed: 34 meters per second
  - Direct-hit damage: 70
  - Splash damage: 35
  - Movement while charging: 55 percent normal speed
  - Cancel cost: Charge progress is lost
- PrimaryAttack:
  - Damage: 18
  - Recovery: 0.28 seconds
  - Purpose: Reliable pressure at close and medium range

[ObservedEvidence]:
- Playtest set A:
  - Skill band: Advanced internal testers
  - Matches: 48
  - HeavyCannon users won 67 percent of rounds
  - HeavyCannon hits occurring within 1.0 seconds after the opponent spent the second Dash charge: 61 percent
- Playtest set B:
  - Skill band: Intermediate internal testers
  - Matches: 52
  - HeavyCannon users won 54 percent of rounds
  - Players reported difficulty knowing when both Dash charges were unavailable
- Review note:
  - Source: ./arena-balance-review.md
  - Finding: Long disengagement chains reduce PrimaryAttack relevance

[BalanceIntent]:
- Dash should remain the strongest immediate repositioning tool
- Spending both Dash charges should create a temporary vulnerability window
- HeavyCannon should reward prediction and commitment
- HeavyCannon should not become a guaranteed punish for every exhausted Dash state
- PrimaryAttack should remain the dependable way to maintain pressure
- Counterplay should become more readable before raw damage is reduced

[ProtectedRelationships]:
- Dash beats committed short-range pressure when a charge is available
- HeavyCannon threatens predictable exits from Dash
- PrimaryAttack pressures opponents who wait indefinitely for ideal HeavyCannon timing
- A player who spends both Dash charges accepts more positional risk than a player who preserves one
- HeavyCannon retains meaningful commitment because canceling loses charge progress

[AdjustmentKnobs]:

  - Distance
  - Recovery
  - Charge visibility
- HeavyCannon:
  - Charge time
  - Projectile speed
  - Damage
  - Movement while charging
- PrimaryAttack:
  - Damage
  - Recovery

[TestMatrix]:
- One Dash charge available against HeavyCannon charge start
- Zero Dash charges available against HeavyCannon charge start
- Two Dash charges available against PrimaryAttack pressure
- HeavyCannon cancel during opponent approach
- Long-range neutral state with both players at full resources
- Corner pressure with defender holding one Dash charge

[Acceptance]:
- Dash remains a credible escape from a committed attack when at least one charge is preserved
- Exhausting both Dash charges creates a readable punish window
- HeavyCannon requires prediction or positional advantage for reliable conversion
- PrimaryAttack remains useful during neutral pressure
- No accepted adjustment removes the commitment cost of HeavyCannon
- Each accepted adjustment is traceable to observed evidence or a protected relationship
